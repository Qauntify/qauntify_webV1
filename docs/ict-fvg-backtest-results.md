# ict_fvg backtest results

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.history_provenance   # prove the data first
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.ict_fvg_history_report
```

Run 2026-09-06 (UTC). Data: Binance monthly archives, **8.96 years**, BTCUSDT
and ETHUSDT, 5m (940,297 bars/symbol) and 15m confluence. Provenance: every
monthly archive individually SHA256-verified against Binance's published
digest at download time (`signals/analysis/history.py:download_month`), the
same guarantee every other report in this repo relies on.

Scored under the corrected fixed-stop R model (`docs/r-model-correction.md`):
one third booked at each of TP1/TP2/TP3, the published stop never moves, so
an unbooked remainder loses its full share when the stop is hit.

## Headline

**`ict_fvg` is not profitable, and unlike every strategy measured before it,
it was already LIVE when this measurement ran** — the pinned strategy for
both `super_scalp` (5m, all symbols) and `xau_scalp` (1m, gold). This is the
first negative long-history verdict in this repository's series to land on a
strategy real users were receiving signals from, not a candidate measured
before being wired up. As a result of this report, both sessions have been
pulled — see "What this means for delivery" below.

| BTCUSD | 8.96 | 940,297 | 25,287 | 62.5% | 20.5% | −0.011R | **−1.419R** | −35,871.8R |
| ETHUSD | 8.96 | 940,297 | 24,788 | 62.3% | 21.2% | −0.009R | **−1.084R** | −26,866.5R |

| n | Gross | **Net** | sd | t | 95% CI | Total |

| 50,075 | −0.010R | **−1.253R** | 2.033 | **−137.94** | **[−1.271, −1.235]** | −62,738.3R |

At 50,075 trades this is not a small-sample question. Gross is close to
flat (win rate 62.5%, roughly matching a coin-flip once the 0.5R/1R/2R
scale-out geometry is accounted for), and the loss is almost entirely a cost
story — see "Reading the result" below.

## Two problems were found and fixed before this number was trustworthy

Two independent issues surfaced while producing this report — one in the
detector, one in the measurement itself. Both are disclosed here rather than
silently folded into a single "corrected" number, per this repo's standard
that a backtest run on a broken measurement is worse than no backtest at all.

### 1. A real detector bug (near-zero-risk setups)

The very first run of this report (before either fix below) produced a
statistically implausible pooled result: **net = −4.346R/trade, sd = 76.9,
worst single trade = −11,460R.** That is not a real trading outcome — a
standard deviation of 77R on a strategy whose gross scale-out ladder tops out
at +2R is a sign the measurement itself is broken, not a sign the strategy is
catastrophic. Per this repo's own standard (a backtest run on a broken
measurement is worse than no backtest — it produces a number that looks
authoritative and isn't), that number was not reported; it was diagnosed
instead.

**Root cause:** `_risk_ok()` in `signals/strategies/ict_fvg/detector.py`
bounded risk only from *above* (`abs(entry - stop) / atr_value <=
MAX_STOP_ATR`), with no floor. The stop is anchored to a swept bar that can
be up to `SWEEP_LOOKBACK` (30) bars in the past, while entry is simply
`candles[-1].close` — the current bar, fully decoupled from the bar the stop
is anchored to. On the rare occasions price drifted back to sit almost
exactly on that stale stop level by the time the setup fired, risk collapsed
toward zero (observed cases: risk/ATR ≈ 0.0001–0.0002, entry and stop
differing in the 3rd–4th decimal, with completely normal ATR readings —
confirmed across multiple unrelated years, ruling out a data-quality
explanation). `cost_r()` divides a price-proportional cost by that risk, so
these near-zero-risk setups produced individual trade costs in the thousands
of R.

**This is not just a backtest artifact — it is a live correctness bug.**
`ict_fvg` was running live at the time; the same decoupled entry/stop
construction could fire in production, and would emit a signal whose
reported "R" (and any position sizing derived from it) is meaningless.

**Fix:** added `MIN_STOP_ATR = 0.15` and made `_risk_ok()` a two-sided check.
5.1% of the raw run's trades (5,697 of 112,105) failed this floor and are
correctly excluded now, not filtered out of the report after the fact —
they never should have been generated by the detector in the first place.
`tests/strategies/test_ict_fvg_detector.py::test_detect_rejects_stop_collapsed_onto_entry`
locks this in by reconstructing the exact degenerate geometry found in the
diagnosis. This is the only code change made before this report ran; no
strategy parameter was tuned for performance.

| | Raw (broken) | After this fix |
|---|---|---|
| Trades | 112,105 | 106,408 |
| Net/trade | −4.346R | −1.235R |
| sd | 76.915 | 1.998 |
| Worst single trade | −11,460.5R | −49.1R |
| Worst 5% of trades' share of total loss | 72% | 30% |

### 2. The backtest wasn't replicating two live-only hard gates

Separately from the detector, `super_scalp` (5m `ict_fvg`) is the only
session in this engine with two extra gates sitting in front of the
detector, in `signals/pipeline/scan.py`, that this report's harness
(`backtest_windowed`) was not simulating:

- `_super_scalp_session_gate` — only scans during London/NY liquid hours
  (`scalp_session_active()`), skipping roughly 42% of the day.
- `_htf_opposes_setup` — hard-rejects a setup whose direction directly
  opposes the 15m HTF trend (the detector itself only treats HTF as a soft
  ordering preference, not a filter — the hard reject lives in the pipeline,
  one layer up).

Both are deterministic and fully reproducible from a candle's timestamp, so
skipping them wasn't a measurement of "can't be done" — it was a gap. It
mattered: 106,408 trades became 50,075 once both gates were applied (the
filters do real work), confirming the earlier number was actually measuring
a strategy variant that doesn't exist live. `backtest_windowed` gained an
optional `gate=` hook for this (`signals/analysis/backtest.py`); no other
strategy's report changes, since none of them have an equivalent hard gate.
The probabilistic LLM confirm step is still not simulated, as it never has
been in any report in this repo — that limitation is unchanged and orthogonal
to this fix.

| | Before this fix | After this fix (current numbers above) |
|---|---|---|
| Trades | 106,408 | 50,075 |
| Gross/trade | −0.026R | −0.010R |
| Net/trade | −1.235R | **−1.253R** |

Gross improved slightly (fewer weak against-trend/illiquid-hours trades
survive), but net barely moved — the cost mechanism doesn't care about
session or trend agreement, so the qualitative verdict is unchanged. This is
reassuring in one sense (the original conclusion wasn't an artifact of the
gap) and unhelpful in another (no free lunch was hiding there either).

## Reading the result

Even after both fixes, net (−1.253R) is far worse than gross (−0.010R) — a
~1.2R/trade cost drag, much larger than any other strategy measured in this
repo. That gap is real, not a residual bug: `cost_r()` charges a fraction of
**price** (20bps round-trip on BTC/ETH) divided by the **absolute dollar
risk**, and `ict_fvg` is the fastest live timeframe (5m) with the tightest
stop geometry (`ATR_STOP_BUFFER = 0.25`, floor now `0.15`× ATR). Sampling the
worst-cost setups directly confirms the mechanism is working as designed, not
malfunctioning:

```
cost_r=81.76  2026-01-10 20:05 UTC  entry=90634.99  stop=90637.21  risk=2.22  atr=12.83  risk/atr=0.173
cost_r=72.42  2023-10-15 09:45 UTC  entry=26897.57  stop=26896.83  risk=0.74  atr=4.89   risk/atr=0.152
cost_r=52.15  2023-07-23 12:45 UTC  entry=29890.00  stop=29888.85  risk=1.15  atr=7.39   risk/atr=0.155
```

(All three land inside London/NY hours and agree with HTF trend — i.e. all
three still fire under the corrected gate.)

Every one of these has a *normal* risk/ATR ratio (0.15–0.25, right where the
detector intends real setups to sit) — the problem is that a $0.74–$2.22
stop distance on a $27,000–$90,000 instrument is inherently uneconomic once a
realistic round-trip fee is applied: the fee alone is 15–80x the defined risk
unit. This is `r_model.py`'s own documented warning ("at these stop
distances cost is a large fraction of 1R, not a rounding error") showing up
in its most extreme form, because 5m BTC/ETH ATR is small in absolute dollars
during quiet regimes while price is not.

Median net (BTCUSD −1.089R, ETHUSD −0.780R) sits above the mean in both
cases — the loss is broad-based, not a few catastrophic trades (worst 10
trades total only −372.1R of BTCUSD's −35,871.8R, ~1% of it; worst 5% of
trades account for ~30% of total loss on both symbols), but the tail is
still fat enough to pull the mean well below the median. That asymmetry is
expected for a stop-loss-scored strategy (every loss caps near −1R minus
cost; wins are capped by the TP3 ladder) and is the same shape seen in every
other losing strategy in this repo — just more pronounced here because of the
timeframe.

| Strategy | Timeframe | Net per trade | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bbma_reentry` | 1h/4h | +0.120R | **profitable** (the one winner) |
| `sr_limit` (maker) | 1h | −0.015R | flat |
| `bbma_extreme` | 1h | −0.019R | losing |
| `cloud_mss` | 15m | −0.046R | losing |
| `bbma_extreme` | short sweep | −0.153R | losing |
| `bbma_reentry` | 15m-equivalent scope | −0.137R | losing |
| `orb_rvol` | 15m | −0.238R | losing badly |
| `sr_zone` | 15m | −0.415R | losing badly |
| **`ict_fvg`** | **5m** | **−1.253R** | **losing badly (pulled from live)** |

`ict_fvg` is the fastest timeframe yet measured in this series and the
result is consistent with the pattern every prior report has found: cost
drag scales with how tight the stop is relative to price, and nothing at
15m-or-faster has been profitable. `ict_fvg` was the worst of the group, and
was the only one running in production at measurement time.

## Chasing profitability: split by confirmation tier

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.ict_fvg_tier_report
```

Before any decision was made, one more honest check was worth running: does
a real edge exist in a *subset* of `ict_fvg`'s current logic, even if the
pooled average doesn't show one? `ict_fvg` fires on three confirmation tiers
of decreasing strength — a full sweep→CHoCH→FVG retest (`choch_fvg`, the
"textbook" ICT entry), a CHoCH without a retest (`choch`), and a bare
sweep-reclaim volume fallback with no CHoCH at all (`sweep_reclaim`). Splitting
the 50,075 gated trades by tier:

| Tier | Share | Gross/trade | Net/trade | Gross t-stat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sweep_reclaim` (weakest) | 61.3% | −0.011R | −1.562R | −2.34 |
| `choch` (mid) | 32.9% | −0.010R | −0.806R | −1.63 |
| `choch_fvg` (strongest) | 5.8% | **+0.007R** | −0.515R | **+0.47** |

This is the theory-predicted shape — the strongest-confirmation tier has the
least cost drag (its stops are structurally wider, since a full sweep→CHoCH→
FVG→retest cycle takes longer to form) and the only non-negative gross
reading. But it settles the question rather than reopening it: **`choch_fvg`'s
gross edge is statistically indistinguishable from zero (t=+0.47, n=2,899) —
there is no real, measurable edge in any subset of this detector's current
logic, not even its best one.** Restricting to `choch_fvg` only would cut net
loss to −0.515R/trade at the cost of a ~94% drop in signal frequency, and
even that is still decisively losing (net t=−23.61). A maker-style resting-
limit entry (cutting the 20bps taker fee to `sr_limit`'s 4bps maker rate)
could plausibly claw further toward breakeven on that tier by arithmetic, but
that's arithmetic, not a measurement, and isn't reported as a result here.

**Conclusion: `ict_fvg` cannot be made profitable at 5m through cost or
quality tuning — it needs a fundamentally different edge, not a patch to this
one.** Continuing to iterate parameters against this same historical sample
looking for a positive number would be curve-fitting, which this report
series has consistently refused to do.

## What this means for delivery

**`ict_fvg` has been pulled from live delivery.** `super_scalp` moved from
`TRADING_SESSIONS` to `AUXILIARY_SESSIONS` in `signals/models.py` (still
declared there so any already-open 5m signal settles with the correct
expiry; just no longer scanned) and `xau_scalp`'s `signals/xau_scan.py`
gained an explicit `XAU_SCALPER_PAUSED = True` short-circuit. Both sessions
now produce zero new signals. `scalp` (`cloud_mss`, 15m) and `swing` (`msnr`,
1h) are unaffected.

The `MIN_STOP_ATR` correctness fix stays regardless — it only rejects setups
that were never economically meaningful, on any timeframe, and would matter
again the moment a replacement 5m/1m strategy reuses similar sweep-anchored
stop logic.

**Finding a real replacement edge for the 5m/1m scalp sessions is out of
scope for this report** and is tracked as a separate future project — the
same brainstorm-a-new-strategy process that produced `cloud_mss`, not a patch
to `ict_fvg`. Until then, `super_scalp` and `xau_scalp` are intentionally
silent rather than running a strategy already proven to have no edge.

## Caveats

- **Crypto only.** Binance lists no gold, so `XAUUSD` (also pinned to both
  `super_scalp` and `xau_scalp`) is not covered by this measurement.
  `signals.analysis.backtest.main` with `STRATEGY_TIMEFRAMES["ict_fvg"]` can
  pull a shorter, live-API-paginated sample for gold, at far less depth —
  not run as part of this report.
- **BTC and ETH are correlated.** The pooled 50,075-trade sample is not
  50,075 independent observations, so the true confidence interval is
  somewhat wider than reported. It does not change the direction of this
  result — a t-statistic of −137.94 has enormous room to narrow and still be
  decisively negative.
- **No profitability tuning was performed.** The only change made to
  `signals/strategies/ict_fvg/` before this report ran was the `MIN_STOP_ATR`
  correctness floor described above, made because the unfixed detector could
  emit signals with meaningless risk — not to move the result in either
  direction.

## Follow-ups

1. **`msnr`** (swing, 1h) is now the only one of the two remaining live
   sessions never measured over long history. Same treatment next.
2. **The broader pattern continues to point at costs, not rules** — `ict_fvg`
   makes it seven for seven at 15m-or-faster with no profitable result. The
   prior for any future fast-timeframe strategy should stay strongly
   negative unless there's a specific reason to expect its cost sensitivity
   differs.
